# Post-MI Care Management Recommendations

## Extracted from OpenEvidence Link 8
URL: https://www.openevidence.com/ask/8e1b7e8a-675b-44d9-8ec4-e1d83e8c923b

### Comprehensive Post-MI Care Management Programs

1. **Structured Multidisciplinary Programs**
   - Implement the Managed Care in Acute Myocardial Infarction (MC-AMI) model
   - Integrate acute intervention, complex revascularization, cardiac rehabilitation, and sudden cardiac death prevention
   - Target reduction in major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events (MACCE)
   - Increase participation in cardiac rehabilitation (CR)
   - Ensure complete revascularization
   - Appropriate use of implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs)
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

2. **Intensive Prevention Programs**
   - Utilize non-physician prevention assistants
   - Implement frequent patient contact (regular phone or mail follow-ups)
   - Focus on improved risk factor control
   - Provide "prevention boosts" to enhance risk factor management (LDL cholesterol and blood pressure)
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

### Cardiac Rehabilitation

1. **Comprehensive Cardiac Rehabilitation Referral**
   - Refer all eligible post-MI patients to comprehensive cardiac rehabilitation
   - Implement as a class I indication (level A or B evidence)
   - Target reductions in all-cause and cardiac mortality (by 20% and 26%, respectively)
   - Reduce hospital readmissions
   - Improve quality of life
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

2. **Core Components of Cardiac Rehabilitation**
   - Supervised exercise training
   - Risk factor modification (lipid, blood pressure, diabetes management)
   - Psychosocial support
   - Patient education
   - Ongoing medical surveillance
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

3. **System-Level Interventions**
   - Implement automated referrals
   - Provide patient navigation services
   - Conduct scalable outreach (mail/phone)
   - Increase participation in underutilized programs
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

### Specific Cardiac Rehabilitation Guidelines

1. **Post-Revascularization Rehabilitation**
   - Prescribe comprehensive home- or center-based CR program before hospital discharge or during first outpatient visit
   - Target reduction in risks of death and hospital readmission
   - Improve quality of life
   - Class I (A) recommendation
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

2. **Exercise-Based CR for STEMI Patients**
   - Recommend exercise-based CR and secondary prevention programs for all patients with STEMI
   - Class I (B) recommendation
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

3. **CR for Unstable Angina/NSTEMI**
   - Refer all eligible patients with acute coronary syndrome or NSTEMI to comprehensive CR program
   - Make referral either before hospital discharge or at first outpatient visit
   - Class I (B) recommendation
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

4. **CR for Heart Transplantation**
   - Recommend CR with aerobic exercise training after heart transplantation
   - Target improvement in exercise capacity and modification of CVD risk factors
   - Encourage 150 min of moderate-intensity exercise per week or 75 min of vigorous-intensity aerobic exercise per week
   - Class I (B) recommendation
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

5. **CR for Chest Pain**
   - For patients with obstructive coronary artery disease with stable chest pain
   - Use exercise treadmill testing to select management strategies
   - Class IIa (B) recommendation
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

6. **CR for Heart Failure**
   - Improve exercise tolerance, functional capacity, and health-related quality of life
   - Class IIa (B) recommendation for general heart failure
   - Class I (A) recommendation for patients with heart failure and exercise training
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

7. **Supervised Exercise for Peripheral Artery Disease**
   - Recommend supervised exercise program for patients with claudication
   - Reduce leg symptoms and improve functional status and quality of life
   - Class I (A) recommendation
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

### Role-Specific Recommendations

1. **Cardiologists**
   - Refer all eligible patients to cardiac rehabilitation before hospital discharge
   - Ensure appropriate use of guideline-directed medical therapy
   - Monitor for and address barriers to medication adherence
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

2. **Primary Care Physicians**
   - Reinforce importance of cardiac rehabilitation attendance
   - Monitor and manage risk factors (lipids, blood pressure, diabetes)
   - Provide ongoing support for lifestyle modifications
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

3. **Cardiac Rehabilitation Specialists**
   - Deliver supervised exercise training programs
   - Provide education on risk factor modification
   - Offer psychosocial support
   - Monitor progress and adjust programs as needed
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

4. **Nurse Care Managers**
   - Coordinate care across multiple providers
   - Monitor medication adherence
   - Provide patient education on disease management
   - Facilitate communication between hospital and outpatient settings
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead

5. **Prevention Assistants**
   - Conduct regular follow-up (phone or mail)
   - Monitor risk factor control
   - Provide reminders for medication adherence
   - Support lifestyle modifications
   - Citation: Cardiac Rehabilitation — Challenges, Advances, and the Road Ahead
