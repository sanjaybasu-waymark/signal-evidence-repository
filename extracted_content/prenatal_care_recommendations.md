# Prenatal Care Management Recommendations

## Extracted from OpenEvidence Link 3
URL: https://www.openevidence.com/ask/61e86959-5aa9-478b-b858-dba876f8db23

### General Prenatal Care Interventions

1. **Early Initiation of Prenatal Care**
   - Begin prenatal care ideally by 10 weeks' gestation
   - Enables accurate pregnancy dating, early risk identification, and prompt initiation of preventive interventions
   - Citation: Prenatal Care: An Evidence-Based Approach

2. **Nutritional Supplementation**
   - Folic acid supplementation (400–800 mcg daily) to reduce neural tube defects
   - Iron supplementation to prevent anemia and associated complications
   - Citation: Update on Prenatal Care

3. **Immunizations**
   - Tdap, influenza, and COVID-19 vaccines to reduce infectious morbidity
   - Citation: Prenatal Care: An Evidence-Based Approach

4. **Universal Screening**
   - Depression, anxiety, intimate partner violence, substance use, and food insecurity early in pregnancy
   - Gestational diabetes (typically at 24–28 weeks), hypertension, and infectious diseases
   - Asymptomatic bacteriuria, sexually transmitted infections, rubella, and varicella immunity
   - Citation: Update on Prenatal Care

5. **Genetic Risk Stratification**
   - Based on family history and ancestry to inform appropriate genetic screening
   - Citation: Preconception Counseling and Care

6. **Lifestyle Counseling**
   - Weight management and exercise tailored to pre-pregnancy BMI
   - Recommendations for appropriate gestational weight gain
   - At least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity exercise
   - Citation: Obesity in Pregnancy

7. **Preeclampsia Prevention**
   - Low-dose aspirin (typically 81 mg daily, starting at 12 weeks) for high-risk patients
   - Multivariate preeclampsia screening at 11-14 weeks to identify high-risk patients
   - Citation: Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine Consult Series #54

### Care Management and Population Health Approaches

1. **Medicaid-managed Pregnancy Medical Home Programs**
   - Risk stratification and intensive care management (≥5 face-to-face visits with a care manager)
   - Screen for psychosocial and medical risk factors
   - Provide targeted support and care coordination
   - Address both clinical and social needs
   - Citation: Prenatal Care: An Evidence-Based Approach

2. **Enhanced Prenatal Care Models**
   - Home visiting, clinical-community linkages, and patient empowerment
   - Improve care participation, increase early prenatal care engagement
   - Reduce emergency department utilization
   - Citation: Update on Prenatal Care

3. **Group Prenatal Care Models**
   - Programs such as Expect With Me
   - Demonstrated improved outcomes, including lower rates of preterm birth, low birthweight, and NICU admissions
   - Citation: Prenatal Care: An Evidence-Based Approach

4. **Telemedicine and Hybrid Models**
   - 4-1-4 plan: 4 in-person visits, 1 ultrasound, 4 virtual visits
   - Maintain maternal and neonatal outcomes while increasing flexibility and patient satisfaction
   - Reduced visit frequency for low-risk patients
   - Home monitoring of blood pressure and weight
   - Citation: Update on Prenatal Care

### Role-Specific Recommendations

1. **Care Coordinators**
   - Conduct comprehensive risk assessments (medical, psychosocial, and social determinants of health)
   - Facilitate timely entry into prenatal care
   - Ensure adherence to evidence-based screening and preventive interventions
   - Coordinate referrals to specialty care and community resources
   - Citation: Prenatal Care: An Evidence-Based Approach

2. **Community Health Workers**
   - Provide culturally concordant health education
   - Conduct home visits
   - Reinforce care plans
   - Identify barriers to care
   - Connect patients to social and health services
   - Improve antenatal care participation, knowledge, and birth outcomes
   - Citation: Update on Prenatal Care

3. **Pharmacists**
   - Counsel on medication safety and adherence
   - Address concerns about teratogenicity
   - Review medication regimens for contraindications in pregnancy
   - Ensure access to essential supplements (e.g., folic acid, iron)
   - Citation: Preconception Counseling and Care

4. **Pharmacy Technicians**
   - Conduct patient outreach
   - Assist with medication access and affordability
   - Manage referrals
   - Schedule appointments
   - Streamline medication management
   - Facilitate communication between patients and the care team
   - Citation: Update on Prenatal Care

5. **Nurse Care Managers**
   - Deliver clinical care coordination
   - Monitor for complications
   - Reinforce anticipatory guidance
   - Provide ongoing risk assessment
   - Collaborate with CHWs and social workers to address both clinical and social needs
   - Citation: Prenatal Care: An Evidence-Based Approach

6. **Social Workers**
   - Screen for and address social determinants (housing, food insecurity, transportation, intimate partner violence)
   - Provide psychosocial support
   - Connect patients to community resources
   - Mitigate nonmedical risks for adverse outcomes
   - Citation: Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine Consult Series #54
