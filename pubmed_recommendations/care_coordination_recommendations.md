# Care Coordination Best Practices Recommendations

## Extracted from: Systematic Review of Care Coordination Interventions Linking Health and Social Services & AHRQ Care Coordination Resources

Citations: 
1. Albertson EM, Chuang E, O'Masta B, et al. Systematic Review of Care Coordination Interventions Linking Health and Social Services for High-Utilizing Patient Populations. Popul Health Manag. 2022;25(1):73-85. doi:10.1089/pop.2021.0057

## Specific Bite-Sized Recommendations

### 1. Structured Needs Assessment Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a standardized needs assessment protocol that includes: 1) Screening for both medical and social needs using validated tools; 2) Documentation of needs in a structured format accessible to the care team; 3) Prioritization of needs based on patient preferences and clinical urgency; 4) Regular reassessment at defined intervals (at minimum quarterly); and 5) Tracking of needs resolution over time.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All patients receiving care coordination services, particularly those with complex medical and social needs

### 2. Individualized Care Planning Process
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a standardized care planning process that includes: 1) Patient-centered goal setting using SMART criteria; 2) Documentation of specific action steps for each goal; 3) Clear assignment of responsibility for each action step; 4) Timeframes for completion; and 5) A process for regular review and updates. Ensure the care plan is accessible to all team members and the patient.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All patients receiving care coordination services, particularly those with multiple chronic conditions or complex care needs

### 3. In-Person Patient Engagement Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a protocol for regular in-person engagement with high-need patients that includes: 1) Initial face-to-face meeting to establish rapport; 2) Minimum monthly in-person contact for high-risk patients; 3) Structured agenda for each visit covering medical updates, social needs, and care plan progress; 4) Documentation of each encounter in a standardized format; and 5) Process for escalation if patient cannot be reached.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** High-utilizing patients with complex medical and social needs

### 4. Active Referral Management System
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement an active referral management system that includes: 1) Direct communication between care coordinator and referral destination; 2) Assistance with appointment scheduling; 3) Preparation of patients for what to expect; 4) Follow-up to confirm appointment completion; 5) Obtaining and reviewing results/recommendations; and 6) Incorporation of findings into the care plan.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients requiring referrals to specialty care, community resources, or social services

### 5. Care Team Communication Structure
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a structured communication system for the care team that includes: 1) Weekly case conferences for complex patients; 2) Standardized templates for case presentation; 3) Documented care team roles and responsibilities; 4) Secure messaging platform for between-meeting communication; and 5) Protocol for urgent issues requiring immediate team attention.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Care coordination teams serving patients with complex needs

### 6. Social Service Integration Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a protocol for integrating social services that includes: 1) Inventory of available community resources by domain (housing, food, transportation, etc.); 2) Established relationships with key social service organizations; 3) Named points of contact at each organization; 4) Standardized referral processes; and 5) Feedback mechanisms to track referral outcomes and service quality.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients with identified social needs affecting health outcomes

### 7. Care Transitions Management Process
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a care transitions management process that includes: 1) Notification system for hospital admissions and discharges; 2) Pre-discharge planning participation; 3) Medication reconciliation within 48 hours of discharge; 4) Home visit within 72 hours for high-risk patients; 5) Follow-up appointment scheduling before discharge; and 6) Communication of transition plan to all relevant providers.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Patients transitioning between care settings, particularly those at high risk for readmission

### 8. Patient Self-Management Support System
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a structured self-management support system that includes: 1) Assessment of patient activation level using validated tools; 2) Tailored education based on activation level; 3) Collaborative action planning for manageable goals; 4) Regular check-ins on progress; 5) Problem-solving support for barriers; and 6) Gradual increase in self-management expectations as skills improve.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All patients with chronic conditions requiring ongoing self-management

### 9. Care Coordinator Staffing Model
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a care coordinator staffing model that includes: 1) Caseload limits based on patient complexity (recommended maximum of 50-75 complex patients per full-time coordinator); 2) Defined minimum qualifications and competencies; 3) Structured onboarding and training program; 4) Regular supervision and case review; and 5) Continuing education requirements specific to care coordination skills.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Organizations implementing care coordination programs

### 10. Health Information Sharing Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a health information sharing protocol that includes: 1) Standardized consent processes for information sharing; 2) Defined essential elements to be shared across settings; 3) Secure methods for information exchange; 4) Regular audits of information completeness and timeliness; and 5) Feedback mechanisms to address information gaps.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All care team members involved in coordinated care delivery

### 11. Care Coordination Quality Measurement System
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a quality measurement system for care coordination that includes: 1) Process measures (e.g., percentage of patients with completed needs assessments); 2) Outcome measures (e.g., hospital utilization rates); 3) Patient experience measures using validated tools; 4) Regular data collection and review cycles; and 5) Quality improvement processes based on measurement findings.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Organizations implementing care coordination programs

### 12. Risk Stratification Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a risk stratification protocol that includes: 1) Algorithm combining clinical, utilization, and social risk factors; 2) Defined risk tiers with corresponding service intensity levels; 3) Regular reassessment of risk status; 4) Process for escalating or de-escalating service intensity based on changing risk; and 5) Documentation of risk level in accessible location for all team members.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patient populations served by care coordination programs

### 13. Community Resource Directory Maintenance
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a process for maintaining an up-to-date community resource directory that includes: 1) Quarterly verification of resource availability and eligibility criteria; 2) Feedback mechanism for care coordinators to report access barriers; 3) Rating system for resource quality and responsiveness; 4) Process for identifying and adding new resources; and 5) Regular training on directory use for all care team members.

**Evidence Level:** C - Limited
**Target Population:** Care coordination teams connecting patients to community resources

### 14. Patient Education Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a standardized patient education protocol that includes: 1) Assessment of learning style and health literacy; 2) Tailored educational materials in appropriate languages and literacy levels; 3) Teach-back method to confirm understanding; 4) Documentation of education provided and comprehension level; and 5) Reinforcement of key concepts at subsequent encounters.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All patients receiving care coordination services

### 15. Care Coordination Technology Infrastructure
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a technology infrastructure for care coordination that includes: 1) Shared care planning platform accessible to all team members; 2) Alert system for emergency department visits and hospitalizations; 3) Task management functionality with due dates and accountability tracking; 4) Reporting capabilities for quality measures; and 5) Mobile access for field-based care coordinators.

**Evidence Level:** C - Limited
**Target Population:** Organizations implementing care coordination programs
