# Post-MI Care Management Recommendations

## Comprehensive Post-MI Care Management Programs

1. **Implement structured, multidisciplinary programs** such as the Managed Care in Acute Myocardial Infarction (MC-AMI) model that integrate acute intervention, complex revascularization, cardiac rehabilitation, scheduled outpatient follow-up, and sudden cardiac death prevention.

2. **Utilize intensive prevention programs** with non-physician prevention assistants and frequent patient contact (e.g., regular phone or mail follow-ups) to improve risk factor control.

3. **Provide short-term "prevention boosts"** to enhance risk factor management, such as LDL cholesterol and blood pressure control, even years after MI.

## Cardiac Rehabilitation

1. **Refer all eligible post-MI patients to comprehensive cardiac rehabilitation** as a class I indication (level A or B evidence), based on robust data showing reductions in all-cause and cardiac mortality, hospital readmissions, and improvements in quality of life.

2. **Ensure core components of cardiac rehabilitation include**:
   - Supervised exercise training
   - Risk factor modification (lipid, blood pressure, diabetes management)
   - Psychosocial support
   - Patient education
   - Ongoing medical surveillance

3. **Implement system-level interventions** such as automated referrals, patient navigation, and scalable outreach (mail/phone) to increase participation in cardiac rehabilitation.

4. **Prescribe a comprehensive home- or center-based CR program** before hospital discharge or during the first outpatient visit for patients who have undergone a revascularization procedure.

5. **Recommend exercise-based CR and secondary prevention programs** for all patients who have had STEMI (ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction).

6. **Refer all eligible patients with an acute coronary syndrome or NSTEMI** (non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction) to a comprehensive CR program, with the referral made either before hospital discharge or at the first outpatient visit.

7. **Recommend CR with aerobic exercise training after heart transplantation**, with a total of 150 min of moderate-intensity exercise per week or 75 min of vigorous-intensity aerobic exercise per week for long-term cardiovascular health.

8. **Utilize exercise treadmill testing** for patients with obstructive coronary artery disease who have stable chest pain despite GDMT (guideline-directed medical therapy) to select management strategies, including CR.

9. **Implement CR programs for patients with heart failure** to improve exercise tolerance, functional capacity, and health-related quality of life.

10. **Provide supervised exercise programs for patients with claudication** to reduce leg symptoms and improve functional status and quality of life.

## Secondary Prevention Medications

1. **Ensure appropriate prescription and adherence to guideline-directed medical therapy**, including:
   - Antiplatelet therapy
   - Beta-blockers
   - ACE inhibitors/ARBs
   - Statins
   - Other lipid-lowering agents as indicated

2. **Implement medication adherence programs** with pharmacist involvement to optimize medication use and address barriers to adherence.

## Risk Factor Management

1. **Establish comprehensive risk factor management programs** targeting:
   - Hypertension control
   - Lipid management
   - Diabetes management
   - Smoking cessation
   - Weight management
   - Physical activity

2. **Utilize nurse- and pharmacist-led interventions** for blood pressure and lipid management to achieve risk factor targets.

## Patient Education and Support

1. **Provide structured education** about MI, risk factors, warning signs, and self-management strategies.

2. **Offer psychosocial support** to address depression, anxiety, and other mental health needs common after MI.

3. **Implement cardiac-specific patient navigation services** to help patients access and navigate the healthcare system.
