# Doula Care Best Practices Recommendations

## Extracted from: The Effect of Doulas on Maternal and Birth Outcomes: A Scoping Review

Citation: Sobczak A, Taylor L, Solomon S, et al. The Effect of Doulas on Maternal and Birth Outcomes: A Scoping Review. Cureus. 2023;15(5):e39451. doi:10.7759/cureus.39451

## Specific Bite-Sized Recommendations

### 1. Continuous Labor Support Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a protocol for doulas to provide continuous, uninterrupted support throughout labor and delivery. This should include: physical presence throughout active labor, consistent emotional reassurance, advocacy for the birthing person's preferences, and techniques to promote comfort and reduce anxiety. Document support provided in 1-2 hour intervals.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All birthing individuals, particularly those from marginalized communities or with limited social support

### 2. Cultural Concordance Matching
**Implementation Guidance:** When possible, match doulas with clients from similar cultural backgrounds or who speak the same language. Establish a diverse doula workforce that reflects the community being served. Create a standardized intake form that assesses cultural preferences, language needs, and specific cultural practices the client wishes to incorporate into their birth experience.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Birthing individuals from minority cultural backgrounds, non-English speakers, and immigrant populations

### 3. Structured Prenatal Education Sessions
**Implementation Guidance:** Conduct a minimum of two structured prenatal education sessions (60-90 minutes each) covering: 1) Birth preferences and creating a birth plan; 2) Comfort measures and coping techniques for labor; 3) Understanding medical interventions and informed consent; 4) Early postpartum and newborn care expectations. Document key concerns and preferences identified during these sessions.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All pregnant individuals, particularly first-time parents and those with limited access to childbirth education

### 4. Specific Comfort Measure Techniques
**Implementation Guidance:** Train doulas to implement specific evidence-based comfort measures including: 1) Counter-pressure techniques for back labor; 2) Position changes every 30-45 minutes during active labor; 3) Guided breathing techniques during contractions; 4) Application of heat/cold therapy to specific areas; 5) Specific massage techniques for different stages of labor. Document which techniques were most effective for future reference.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Laboring individuals, particularly those attempting unmedicated births or those experiencing prolonged labor

### 5. Breastfeeding Initiation Support
**Implementation Guidance:** Provide immediate postpartum breastfeeding support within the first hour after birth by: 1) Assisting with proper positioning and latch; 2) Educating on hunger cues; 3) Demonstrating hand expression techniques; 4) Supporting skin-to-skin contact; 5) Documenting initial feeding session and any challenges encountered. Follow up within 24-48 hours to address early breastfeeding challenges.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All breastfeeding parents, particularly first-time parents and those from communities with lower breastfeeding rates

### 6. Structured Postpartum Follow-up Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured postpartum follow-up protocol consisting of: 1) In-person home visit within 3-5 days postpartum; 2) Phone/virtual check-in at 1-2 weeks; 3) Final follow-up at 4-6 weeks. During each contact, use a standardized assessment tool to evaluate physical recovery, emotional wellbeing, infant feeding, and resource needs. Provide referrals to appropriate services for identified concerns.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All postpartum individuals, particularly those with limited social support or at risk for postpartum mood disorders

### 7. Birth Trauma Prevention Techniques
**Implementation Guidance:** Train doulas to implement specific techniques to reduce birth trauma, including: 1) Maintaining continuous communication about what is happening during labor; 2) Requesting time for clients to process information before procedures; 3) Encouraging verbalization of fears and concerns; 4) Facilitating communication between client and medical staff; 5) Supporting informed decision-making through specific question prompts.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All birthing individuals, particularly those with previous traumatic births, history of sexual trauma, or anxiety disorders

### 8. Advocacy Communication Framework
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured communication framework for doulas to advocate effectively, using the BRAIN acronym (Benefits, Risks, Alternatives, Intuition, Nothing) when discussing interventions. Train doulas to use specific phrases such as "My client would like more information about..." or "Could we have a moment to discuss this option?" Document instances where advocacy was needed and outcomes.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All birthing individuals, particularly those from marginalized communities or with communication barriers

### 9. Partner/Support Person Inclusion Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a protocol for actively involving the partner or other support persons in the birth process: 1) Teach specific support techniques they can provide; 2) Create defined roles that complement rather than replace the doula's role; 3) Include partners in prenatal education sessions; 4) Provide specific guidance on physical and emotional support techniques; 5) Facilitate bonding opportunities with the newborn.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Partners and support persons of birthing individuals

### 10. Perinatal Mood Disorder Screening
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement routine screening for perinatal mood disorders using validated tools (e.g., Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale) during prenatal visits and postpartum follow-ups. Establish clear referral pathways for clients who screen positive, including warm handoffs to mental health providers. Document screening results and referrals made.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All pregnant and postpartum individuals, particularly those with history of mental health conditions or limited social support

### 11. Birth Preference Documentation
**Implementation Guidance:** Create a standardized but customizable birth preferences document that includes: 1) Preferences for labor environment (lighting, sound, movement); 2) Comfort measure preferences; 3) Medical intervention preferences; 4) Emergency contingency preferences; 5) Immediate postpartum preferences. Review this document with healthcare providers at 36-38 weeks gestation and bring copies to the birth facility.

**Evidence Level:** C - Limited
**Target Population:** All pregnant individuals planning hospital births

### 12. Community Resource Connection
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop and maintain a comprehensive, up-to-date database of community resources for new parents including: WIC offices, lactation support, diaper banks, food assistance, mental health services, and parent support groups. Provide personalized referrals based on specific client needs identified during prenatal or postpartum visits. Document resources provided and follow up on utilization.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All pregnant and postpartum individuals, particularly those with socioeconomic challenges or limited resource awareness
