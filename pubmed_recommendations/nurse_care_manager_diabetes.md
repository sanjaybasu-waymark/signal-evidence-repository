# Nurse Care Manager Diabetes Interventions

## Extracted from: Diabetes Nurse Case Management and Motivational Interviewing for Change (DYNAMIC)

Citation: Stuckey HL, Dellasega C, Graber NJ, Mauger DT, Lendel I, Gabbay RA. Diabetes Nurse Case Management and Motivational Interviewing for Change (DYNAMIC): Study Design and Baseline Characteristics in the Chronic Care Model for Type 2 Diabetes. Contemp Clin Trials. 2009 Mar 26;30(4):366-374. doi: 10.1016/j.cct.2009.03.002.

## Specific Bite-Sized Recommendations

### 1. Patient Registry System for Visit Preparation
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a computerized patient registry system that allows nurse care managers to print a single summary sheet prior to each patient visit. This sheet should include:
- Self-care goals
- Clinical parameters over time (BP, HbA1C, LDL)
- Dates of last ophthalmologic exam
- Aspirin use status
- Foot exam status
- Specific prompts for issues to be addressed at the current visit

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Medicaid-enrolled individuals with type 2 diabetes

### 2. Standing Orders Protocol for Screening Tests
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish standing orders for nurse care managers to implement clinical practice guidelines without requiring physician approval for each instance, including:
- HbA1C testing (quarterly for uncontrolled patients)
- LDL testing (annually or more frequently if elevated)
- Nephropathy screening (annual urine microalbumin)
- Yearly ophthalmologic exam referrals
- Performance of comprehensive foot exams

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Medicaid-enrolled individuals with type 2 diabetes

### 3. Structured Follow-Up Visit Schedule
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured follow-up schedule for nurse care managers to meet individually with patients:
- Initial visit (baseline assessment)
- 2-week follow-up
- 6-week follow-up
- 3-month follow-up
- 6-month follow-up
- 12-month follow-up
- Minimum of every 6 months thereafter
- Supplement with telephone and email correspondence between visits

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Medicaid-enrolled individuals with type 2 diabetes, particularly those with uncontrolled values

### 4. Specific Motivational Interviewing Questions for Diabetes Self-Management
**Implementation Guidance:** Use these specific motivational interviewing questions during patient encounters:
1. "What concerns you most about your diabetes?"
2. "On a scale of 1-10, how important is it for you to change [specific behavior]?"
3. "Why did you choose that number and not a lower one?"
4. "What would it take to move you from a [current number] to a [higher number]?"
5. "What are the good things about changing this behavior?"
6. "What are the not-so-good things about changing this behavior?"
7. "What has worked for you in the past when you've tried to make a change?"
8. "What specific steps do you feel ready to take in the next week?"

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Medicaid-enrolled individuals with type 2 diabetes, particularly those struggling with behavior change

### 5. Problem Areas in Diabetes (PAID) Assessment
**Implementation Guidance:** Administer the Problem Areas in Diabetes (PAID) scale to assess diabetes-related emotional distress. This 20-item questionnaire helps identify specific areas of diabetes management causing emotional burden. Use results to:
- Identify patients with high distress scores (>40)
- Target specific areas of diabetes management causing the most distress
- Track changes in emotional distress over time
- Guide conversations about emotional aspects of diabetes management

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Medicaid-enrolled individuals with type 2 diabetes, particularly those showing signs of diabetes distress or depression

### 6. Clinical Care Guidelines Collaboration with Primary Care
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a structured process for nurse care managers to collaborate with primary care providers by:
1. Documenting clinical and management issues identified during patient visits
2. Providing specific guideline recommendations for diabetes management
3. Sharing depression screening results and treatment recommendations
4. Creating a standardized communication template for consistent information sharing
5. Following up on recommendations that haven't been implemented

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Healthcare teams serving Medicaid-enrolled individuals with type 2 diabetes
