# Prenatal Care Management Recommendations

## Evidence-Based Prenatal Care Interventions

1. **Initiate prenatal care early** - ideally by 10 weeks' gestation - and ensure adherence to evidence-based protocols to reduce maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality.

2. **Provide folic acid supplementation** (400-800 mcg daily) to reduce neural tube defects.

3. **Recommend iron supplementation** to prevent anemia and associated complications.

4. **Administer appropriate immunizations** including Tdap, influenza, and COVID-19 vaccines to reduce infectious morbidity.

5. **Implement universal screening** for depression, anxiety, intimate partner violence, substance use, and food insecurity early in pregnancy.

6. **Conduct routine screening** for gestational diabetes (typically at 24-28 weeks), hypertension, and infectious diseases (e.g., asymptomatic bacteriuria, sexually transmitted infections, rubella, and varicella immunity).

7. **Perform genetic risk stratification** based on family history and ancestry to inform appropriate genetic screening.

## Lifestyle and Preventive Interventions

1. **Provide tailored lifestyle counseling and weight management** based on pre-pregnancy BMI, with recommendations for appropriate gestational weight gain.

2. **Recommend at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity exercise** to prevent excessive weight gain associated with adverse outcomes.

3. **Prescribe low-dose aspirin** (typically 81 mg daily, starting at 12 weeks) for high-risk patients, such as those with a history of preeclampsia or chronic hypertension, to reduce preeclampsia risk.

## Care Management and Population Health Approaches

1. **Implement Medicaid-managed Pregnancy Medical Home programs** that incorporate risk stratification and intensive care management (≥5 face-to-face visits with a care manager) to reduce preterm birth and low birthweight.

2. **Screen for psychosocial and medical risk factors** and provide targeted support and care coordination, addressing both clinical and social needs.

3. **Utilize enhanced prenatal care models** including home visiting, clinical-community linkages, and patient empowerment to improve care participation and reduce emergency department utilization.

4. **Consider group prenatal care models** such as Expect With Me, which have demonstrated improved outcomes including lower rates of preterm birth, low birthweight, and NICU admissions compared to individual care.

## Role-Specific Interventions

### Care Coordinators
1. **Conduct comprehensive risk assessments** (medical, psychosocial, and social determinants of health)
2. **Facilitate timely entry into prenatal care**
3. **Ensure adherence to evidence-based screening and preventive interventions**
4. **Coordinate referrals to specialty care and community resources**

### Community Health Workers
1. **Provide culturally concordant health education**
2. **Conduct home visits**
3. **Reinforce care plans**
4. **Identify barriers to care**
5. **Connect patients to social and health services**

### Pharmacists
1. **Counsel on medication safety and adherence**
2. **Address concerns about teratogenicity**
3. **Review medication regimens for contraindications in pregnancy**
4. **Ensure access to essential supplements** (e.g., folic acid, iron)

### Pharmacy Technicians
1. **Conduct patient outreach**
2. **Assist with medication access and affordability**
3. **Manage referrals**
4. **Schedule appointments**

### Nurse Care Managers
1. **Deliver clinical care coordination**
2. **Monitor for complications**
3. **Reinforce anticipatory guidance**
4. **Provide ongoing risk assessment**
5. **Collaborate with CHWs and social workers** to address both clinical and social needs

### Social Workers
1. **Screen for and address social determinants** (housing, food insecurity, transportation, intimate partner violence)
2. **Provide psychosocial support**
3. **Connect patients to community resources**

## Innovative and Flexible Care Delivery

1. **Utilize telemedicine and hybrid prenatal care models** (e.g., the 4-1-4 plan: 4 in-person visits, 1 ultrasound, 4 virtual visits) to maintain outcomes while increasing flexibility and patient satisfaction.

2. **Consider reduced visit frequency for low-risk patients** as this does not compromise outcomes.

3. **Implement home monitoring of blood pressure and weight** for patients with medical comorbidities to reduce clinic utilization.

4. **Integrate multivariate preeclampsia screening** (combining maternal, biophysical, and biochemical markers at 11-14 weeks) to identify high-risk patients who benefit from prophylactic low-dose aspirin and closer monitoring.

5. **Conduct ongoing assessment of social determinants** and connect patients to community resources (housing, transportation, food security).
