## Specific Bite-Sized Recommendations

### 1. Structured Dialectical Behavior Therapy Skills Training
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured DBT skills training protocol that includes four specific modules: 1) Mindfulness skills (focusing on "what" skills of observing, describing, and participating, and "how" skills of being nonjudgmental, one-mindful, and effective); 2) Distress tolerance skills (including crisis survival strategies such as TIPP, ACCEPTS, and self-soothing techniques); 3) Emotion regulation skills (identifying emotions, reducing vulnerability to emotional mind, and increasing positive emotions); and 4) Interpersonal effectiveness skills (using DEAR MAN, GIVE, and FAST skills for relationship management). Each module should be taught sequentially with specific homework assignments to practice skills between sessions.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Individuals with emotional dysregulation, particularly those with borderline personality disorder, substance use disorders, or chronic suicidality

### 2. Single-Session Growth Mindset Intervention
**Implementation Guidance:** Deliver a focused single-session intervention targeting growth mindset that includes: 1) Presenting scientific evidence that personality, emotions, and behaviors can change over time; 2) Having clients read and reflect on 2-3 brief testimonials from peers who have overcome similar challenges; 3) Guiding clients to write a brief letter to someone else struggling with similar problems, explaining how these challenges can improve with effort and support; and 4) Helping clients identify one specific action step they can take in the next week. The entire intervention should be completed in 30-45 minutes and documented with pre-post measures of hope and agency.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Adolescents and adults with depression, anxiety, or low motivation for change

### 3. Cognitive Restructuring Protocol for Negative Automatic Thoughts
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured cognitive restructuring protocol that includes: 1) Teaching clients to identify negative automatic thoughts using thought records that document situations, emotions, and thoughts; 2) Training in recognizing common cognitive distortions (catastrophizing, all-or-nothing thinking, mind reading, etc.); 3) Guiding clients to evaluate evidence for and against each thought using specific prompting questions; 4) Developing balanced alternative thoughts that acknowledge both positive and negative aspects of situations; and 5) Practicing applying these skills to increasingly challenging situations. Document changes in belief strength for targeted thoughts on a 0-100 scale.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Individuals with depression, anxiety disorders, or negative thought patterns

### 4. Behavioral Activation Intervention for Depression
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a behavioral activation protocol that includes: 1) Monitoring daily activities and corresponding mood states using a structured activity log; 2) Identifying patterns of avoidance and withdrawal; 3) Scheduling pleasant and mastery activities that align with the client's values; 4) Gradually increasing activity level through specific, measurable weekly goals; and 5) Evaluating and addressing barriers to activation. The intervention should include at least 3 activities per day that provide either pleasure or a sense of accomplishment, with daily mood ratings to track progress.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Individuals with depression, particularly those with low motivation or anhedonia

### 5. Dialectical Chain Analysis for Problem Behaviors
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured dialectical chain analysis protocol for problem behaviors that includes: 1) Precisely defining the specific problem behavior; 2) Identifying the precipitating event that started the chain; 3) Identifying vulnerability factors that made the person susceptible (lack of sleep, poor nutrition, substance use); 4) Detailing links in the chain including thoughts, emotions, body sensations, and actions; 5) Identifying consequences of the behavior; and 6) Developing solution analysis with specific alternative behaviors at each link. Document the analysis in writing and review in subsequent sessions to identify patterns.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Individuals with self-destructive behaviors, emotional dysregulation, or impulsivity

### 6. Strengths-Based Single-Session Assessment and Intervention
**Implementation Guidance:** Conduct a focused single-session assessment and intervention that includes: 1) Asking the client to identify their most pressing concern to address today; 2) Conducting a brief assessment focused specifically on that concern; 3) Identifying client strengths and past successes relevant to the current problem; 4) Collaboratively developing 2-3 concrete action steps that build on identified strengths; 5) Anticipating and problem-solving potential barriers; and 6) Providing a written summary of the plan. Follow up with a brief phone check-in within one week to reinforce action steps and problem-solve barriers.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Individuals seeking brief intervention, those with limited access to ongoing care, or as an entry point to longer-term services

### 7. Mindfulness-Based Emotional Awareness Training
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured mindfulness-based emotional awareness protocol that includes: 1) Teaching clients to identify physical sensations associated with different emotions; 2) Practicing non-judgmental observation of emotions as they arise and change; 3) Guiding clients through specific mindfulness exercises focused on emotional awareness (e.g., mindful breathing while noticing emotions, body scan with emotional awareness); 4) Developing skills to describe emotions in specific, non-judgmental language; and 5) Practicing staying present with difficult emotions without acting on them. Sessions should include at least 10 minutes of guided mindfulness practice and daily homework assignments of 5-10 minutes of practice.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Individuals with emotional dysregulation, avoidance of emotions, or difficulty identifying feelings

### 8. Crisis Survival Skills Training Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a crisis survival skills training protocol that includes: 1) Teaching the TIPP skills (Temperature change, Intense exercise, Paced breathing, Progressive muscle relaxation) for managing acute distress; 2) Developing personalized distraction techniques using the ACCEPTS framework (Activities, Contributing, Comparisons, Emotions, Pushing away, Thoughts, Sensations); 3) Creating individualized self-soothing kits that engage all five senses; 4) Practicing pros and cons analysis for crisis situations; and 5) Developing a written crisis plan that includes specific skills to use at different levels of distress. Provide clients with pocket cards summarizing key skills and encourage daily practice of at least one skill.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Individuals with suicidal ideation, self-harm behaviors, or difficulty managing crisis situations

### 9. Exposure Therapy Protocol for Anxiety
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured exposure therapy protocol that includes: 1) Collaboratively developing a fear hierarchy with 8-10 situations ranked from least to most anxiety-provoking (0-100 scale); 2) Teaching anxiety management skills including diaphragmatic breathing and cognitive reframing; 3) Conducting in-session exposure exercises starting with moderate-anxiety items (around 40-50 on the scale); 4) Assigning between-session exposure homework with detailed monitoring forms; and 5) Gradually progressing up the hierarchy as mastery is achieved at each level. Document subjective units of distress (SUDS) before, during, and after each exposure exercise.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Individuals with anxiety disorders, phobias, or avoidance behaviors

### 10. Interpersonal Effectiveness Skills Training
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement an interpersonal effectiveness skills training protocol that includes: 1) Teaching the DEAR MAN skills for objective effectiveness (Describe, Express, Assert, Reinforce, Mindful, Appear confident, Negotiate); 2) Teaching the GIVE skills for relationship effectiveness (Gentle, Interested, Validate, Easy manner); 3) Teaching the FAST skills for self-respect effectiveness (Fair, Apologies avoided, Stick to values, Truthful); 4) Role-playing application of these skills to real-life situations; and 5) Assigning homework to practice skills in increasingly challenging interpersonal situations. Document specific interpersonal situations and which skills were applied.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Individuals with interpersonal difficulties, assertiveness deficits, or relationship conflicts

### 11. Validation Strategies for Therapeutic Relationships
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured validation approach that includes six specific levels: 1) Active listening with full attention; 2) Accurate reflection of what the client has said; 3) Articulating the unspoken or "reading between the lines"; 4) Validating in terms of past learning or biological factors; 5) Normalizing or recognizing the current validity of the client's responses; and 6) Radical genuineness and treating the client as valid, capable, and equal. Document which validation strategies were used in each session and their observed impact on the therapeutic relationship.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All clients, particularly those with histories of invalidation, trauma, or difficulty trusting others

### 12. Problem-Solving Therapy Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured problem-solving therapy protocol that includes: 1) Defining a specific problem in concrete, behavioral terms; 2) Setting a specific, measurable goal related to the problem; 3) Brainstorming multiple potential solutions without evaluating them; 4) Evaluating the pros and cons of each potential solution; 5) Selecting and implementing the most promising solution with a detailed action plan; and 6) Evaluating outcomes and adjusting as needed. Document each step in writing and review progress at subsequent sessions.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Individuals with depression, executive functioning difficulties, or those facing concrete life challenges

### 13. Emotion Regulation Skills Training
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement an emotion regulation skills training protocol that includes: 1) Teaching clients to identify and label emotions accurately; 2) Reducing vulnerability to negative emotions through the PLEASE skills (treating PhysicaL illness, balanced Eating, avoiding mood-Altering drugs, balanced Sleep, and getting Exercise); 3) Increasing positive emotional experiences through pleasant events scheduling; 4) Taking opposite action to emotions when they aren't justified by the facts; and 5) Applying mindfulness to current emotions without judgment. Assign daily emotion monitoring using structured logs that track emotions, triggers, and responses.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Individuals with emotional dysregulation, mood disorders, or difficulty managing specific emotions

### 14. Cognitive Processing Therapy for Trauma
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured cognitive processing therapy protocol that includes: 1) Education about trauma reactions and the cognitive model; 2) Written trauma narratives with detailed sensory and emotional components; 3) Identification of stuck points (problematic thoughts related to the trauma); 4) Challenging stuck points using Socratic questioning and evidence evaluation; 5) Addressing themes of safety, trust, power/control, esteem, and intimacy; and 6) Developing more balanced beliefs in each theme area. Document identified stuck points and evidence for and against each one.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Individuals with post-traumatic stress disorder or trauma-related symptoms

### 15. Single-Session Family Consultation Model
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a single-session family consultation model that includes: 1) Brief pre-session questionnaire identifying the family's most pressing concern; 2) Clarifying what would make the session most helpful; 3) Gathering information from each family member's perspective; 4) Identifying family strengths and resources; 5) Providing specific, actionable recommendations based on evidence-based family interventions; and 6) Developing a concrete action plan with 2-3 specific steps. Provide a written summary of recommendations and follow up within two weeks to assess implementation and provide additional guidance if needed.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Families seeking brief consultation, those with limited access to ongoing care, or as an entry point to longer-term services
