# Food Insecurity Recommendations

## Screening and Identification

1. **Implement validated screening tools**:
   - Use the two-item Hunger Vital Sign (sensitivity >95%, specificity >82%)
   - Consider USDA Household Food Security Survey (HFSS) subsets for more detailed assessment
   - Integrate screening into electronic health records and clinical workflows

2. **Adopt trauma-informed screening approaches**:
   - Train staff on culturally sensitive screening methods
   - Create non-stigmatizing screening environments
   - Use empathetic, person-centered language

3. **Establish systematic screening protocols**:
   - Screen at intake, annual visits, and when risk factors are present
   - Document food insecurity status in patient records
   - Create clear workflows for positive screens

## Direct Food Access Interventions

1. **Implement medically tailored meals programs**:
   - Provide meals designed for specific nutritional needs of patients with chronic diseases
   - Target patients with conditions sensitive to dietary management (diabetes, heart failure, etc.)
   - Include delivery services for patients with mobility or transportation challenges

2. **Develop food prescription programs**:
   - Create "prescription" vouchers for fruits, vegetables, and healthy foods
   - Partner with local farmers markets and grocery stores for redemption
   - Track redemption rates and dietary improvements

3. **Establish clinic-based food pantries**:
   - Create onsite food distribution centers in healthcare facilities
   - Stock culturally appropriate, nutritious foods
   - Provide emergency food boxes for immediate needs

4. **Distribute produce vouchers**:
   - Implement voucher programs for fresh produce
   - Partner with local agricultural programs
   - Combine with nutrition education for maximum impact

## Care Coordination and Navigation

1. **Integrate food resource navigation**:
   - Assign dedicated staff for food resource coordination
   - Create resource directories of local food assistance programs
   - Develop referral pathways to community food resources

2. **Assist with federal nutrition program enrollment**:
   - Help patients apply for SNAP, WIC, and other benefits
   - Address barriers to enrollment (documentation, language, etc.)
   - Follow up to ensure successful enrollment

3. **Implement closed-loop referral systems**:
   - Track referrals to food resources
   - Follow up on utilization and outcomes
   - Adjust interventions based on patient feedback

## Education and Skill Building

1. **Provide nutrition education**:
   - Offer classes on healthy eating on a budget
   - Develop culturally appropriate educational materials
   - Address specific dietary needs for chronic conditions

2. **Implement cooking skills programs**:
   - Teach preparation of affordable, nutritious meals
   - Provide recipes using commonly available ingredients
   - Consider virtual or in-person demonstration formats

3. **Offer financial counseling**:
   - Provide budgeting assistance for food purchases
   - Connect to financial literacy resources
   - Address broader financial stability issues

## Health System Integration

1. **Develop food insecurity care pathways**:
   - Create standardized protocols for addressing food insecurity
   - Integrate food security interventions into chronic disease management
   - Include food security in care planning and documentation

2. **Train healthcare providers**:
   - Educate clinical staff on food insecurity impacts and interventions
   - Develop competencies for addressing food needs
   - Include food insecurity in continuing education

3. **Measure outcomes and impact**:
   - Track food security status over time
   - Monitor health outcomes (HbA1c, blood pressure, etc.)
   - Assess healthcare utilization and cost impacts

## Community and Policy Engagement

1. **Develop community partnerships**:
   - Collaborate with food banks and community organizations
   - Create formal referral relationships with food assistance programs
   - Participate in local food policy councils

2. **Advocate for policy improvements**:
   - Support expansion of federal nutrition programs
   - Advocate for increased SNAP benefits and eligibility
   - Engage in efforts to address structural inequities

3. **Implement sustainable funding models**:
   - Explore healthcare payer reimbursement for food interventions
   - Develop business cases demonstrating return on investment
   - Seek grant funding and philanthropic support

## Role-Specific Recommendations

### Care Coordinators and Nurse Care Managers

1. **Conduct routine food insecurity screening** using validated tools
2. **Develop individualized food security plans** as part of care management
3. **Coordinate referrals to food resources** and track outcomes
4. **Integrate food security goals** into chronic disease management plans

### Community Health Workers (CHWs)

1. **Provide culturally appropriate food resource navigation**
2. **Assist with SNAP and WIC applications** in community settings
3. **Conduct home visits to assess food needs** and storage capabilities
4. **Connect patients to neighborhood food resources**

### Social Workers

1. **Address multiple social determinants** affecting food security
2. **Provide case management** for complex food insecurity cases
3. **Advocate for patients** with federal nutrition program agencies
4. **Coordinate with housing and financial assistance** programs

### Primary Care Providers

1. **Incorporate food security assessment** into routine care
2. **Consider food access** when prescribing medications and treatments
3. **Write "prescriptions" for food resources** when appropriate
4. **Document food insecurity** as a diagnosis or problem

### Dietitians

1. **Develop meal plans** accounting for food access limitations
2. **Provide practical nutrition counseling** for limited resources
3. **Adapt dietary recommendations** to available foods
4. **Lead cooking demonstrations** and nutrition education
