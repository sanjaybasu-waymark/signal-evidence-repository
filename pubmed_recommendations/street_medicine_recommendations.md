# Street Medicine Best Practices Recommendations

## Extracted from: Street Medicine Best Practices Document

Citation: Mental Health Services Oversight and Accountability Commission & Social Finance, Inc. References and Best Practices for Delivering "Street Medicine". May 2020. https://bhsoac.ca.gov/sites/default/files/2020-07/Street%20medicine%20references%20and%20best%20practices%20vshared.pdf

## Specific Bite-Sized Recommendations

### 1. Place-Based Team Structure
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a place-based team structure with consistent schedules to engage the same individuals on a recurring basis. Establish regular visiting times at specific locations where unsheltered individuals congregate to build trust and continuity of care.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Unsheltered individuals experiencing homelessness

### 2. Relationship-Based Care Model
**Implementation Guidance:** Adopt a "relationship-based" care model requiring frequent and recurring contact with patients. Focus on building rapport before attempting medical interventions, and maintain consistent outreach workers to foster trust with individuals who may have experienced trauma in healthcare settings.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Unsheltered individuals experiencing homelessness, particularly those with previous negative healthcare experiences

### 3. Multi-Disciplinary Team Composition
**Implementation Guidance:** Form multi-disciplinary street medicine teams that include outreach workers, medical professionals (nurses/physician assistants), behavioral health specialists, and housing specialists. Ensure each team member has clearly defined roles while maintaining flexibility to address immediate needs.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Unsheltered individuals experiencing homelessness with complex medical, behavioral, and social needs

### 4. Consistent Presence Approach
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a "show up — again and again and again" approach to care delivery. Maintain regular presence even when individuals initially refuse help, as consistent presence is itself a therapeutic intervention that builds trust over time.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Unsheltered individuals experiencing homelessness, particularly those resistant to care

### 5. Moment-Focused Care
**Implementation Guidance:** Focus on addressing immediate needs in the moment rather than developing complex long-term care plans that may overwhelm patients. Prioritize the most pressing health concerns identified by the patient rather than imposing a clinician-determined hierarchy of needs.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Unsheltered individuals experiencing homelessness with multiple competing needs

### 6. Field-Based Documentation System
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a streamlined, field-based documentation system that allows for real-time recording of patient encounters, medications prescribed, and follow-up needs. Use mobile technology that functions without consistent internet access and synchronizes when connectivity is available.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Street medicine providers serving unsheltered populations

### 7. Adapted Clinical Guidelines for Street Conditions
**Implementation Guidance:** Utilize adapted clinical guidelines specifically designed for street-sleeping populations for common conditions including asthma, COPD, hypertension, frostbite, immersion foot, and wound care. Modify standard treatment protocols to account for limited resources, storage capabilities, and follow-up challenges.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Clinicians providing care to unsheltered individuals

### 8. Harm Reduction Approach
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement harm reduction strategies for substance use disorders, including distribution of naloxone, clean supplies, and education on safer use practices. Focus on reducing negative consequences of substance use rather than requiring abstinence as a precondition for receiving care.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Unsheltered individuals with substance use disorders

### 9. Trauma-Informed Engagement
**Implementation Guidance:** Utilize trauma-informed approaches when engaging unsheltered individuals by: 1) Asking permission before approaching someone's living space; 2) Clearly identifying yourself and your role; 3) Offering choices in how care is delivered; 4) Avoiding clinical jargon; 5) Respecting autonomy in decision-making; and 6) Acknowledging past negative experiences with healthcare systems.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Unsheltered individuals experiencing homelessness, particularly those with trauma histories

### 10. Medication Management Adaptations
**Implementation Guidance:** Adapt medication regimens for street conditions by: 1) Prescribing once-daily dosing when possible; 2) Using blister packs or other weather-resistant packaging; 3) Providing small quantities that can be easily carried; 4) Considering medication storage requirements (temperature, security); and 5) Selecting medications with minimal side effects that could compromise safety in unsheltered environments.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Unsheltered individuals requiring medication management

### 11. Coordinated Entry System Integration
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish formal connections between street medicine teams and the local homeless Coordinated Entry System. Train street medicine providers to complete housing assessments in the field and directly input data into the housing prioritization system to streamline access to housing resources.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Unsheltered individuals experiencing homelessness seeking housing

### 12. Weather-Based Outreach Protocols
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop specific outreach protocols for extreme weather conditions (heat, cold, rain, snow). Include: 1) Maps of high-risk encampment areas; 2) Stockpiles of weather-appropriate supplies; 3) Transportation plans to move vulnerable individuals to shelter; 4) Screening tools for weather-related conditions; and 5) Extended outreach hours during weather emergencies.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Unsheltered individuals at risk during extreme weather events
