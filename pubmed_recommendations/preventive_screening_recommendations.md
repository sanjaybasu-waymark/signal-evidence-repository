# Preventive Screening Recommendations

## Patient-Focused Interventions

### Patient Navigation

1. **Implement patient navigation programs** for vulnerable and high-risk groups:
   - Assist individuals in overcoming logistical barriers (transportation, scheduling)
   - Address linguistic and cultural barriers
   - Provide education about screening importance and procedures
   - Facilitate scheduling and follow-up

2. **Tailor navigation approaches** to address specific patient barriers:
   - Language-specific navigation for non-English speakers
   - Transportation assistance for those with mobility or access challenges
   - Cultural adaptation for diverse populations

3. **Integrate patient navigation with population health IT systems** to systematically identify and track patients needing screening.

### Personalized Outreach

1. **Implement direct mail of FIT/FOBT kits** for colorectal cancer screening:
   - Include low-literacy instructions
   - Provide postage-paid return envelopes
   - Follow up with reminders

2. **Establish multi-modal outreach systems**:
   - Send personalized letters
   - Make telephone reminders (personal or automated)
   - Use text message reminders
   - Combine multiple modalities for enhanced effectiveness

3. **Tailor outreach materials** to address health literacy, language, and cultural needs.

### Patient Education

1. **Deliver targeted education** via:
   - Brochures
   - Videos
   - Risk counseling sessions

2. **Implement shared decision-making models** like Project ACTIVE:
   - Engage patients in prioritizing preventive care goals
   - Develop personalized action plans
   - Set specific, achievable screening targets

### Financial Incentives

1. **Consider modest financial incentives** for screening completion, particularly for populations with financial barriers.

2. **Prioritize other interventions** (navigation, outreach) over financial incentives when resources are limited.

## Provider and Health System Interventions

### Provider Recommendation and Counseling

1. **Train providers to deliver clear, strong recommendations** for preventive screenings.

2. **Combine provider counseling with patient education and reminders** for maximum impact.

3. **Ensure consistent messaging** across all members of the care team.

### Organizational Changes

1. **Establish dedicated prevention clinics** focused on screening and preventive care.

2. **Assign non-physician staff** (medical assistants, navigators) to manage preventive care.

3. **Implement standing orders** for preventive screenings.

4. **Redesign clinical workflows** to prioritize preventive care.

### Health Information Technology

1. **Utilize EHR-based tools** to identify patients overdue for screening.

2. **Implement population health IT systems** to track outreach efforts and monitor progress.

3. **Establish provider reminders and audit-and-feedback mechanisms** to improve screening rates.

4. **Use practice facilitation and academic detailing** to support implementation of evidence-based interventions.

### Multifaceted, Multilevel Interventions

1. **Combine interventions** that address barriers at patient, provider, and system levels.

2. **Tailor interventions** to the needs of populations facing multiple structural barriers.

3. **Implement comprehensive approaches** that include mailed outreach, patient navigation, and automated reminders.

## Role-Specific Interventions

### Care Coordinators

1. **Identify patients overdue for screening** using registries or EHR dashboards.

2. **Conduct targeted outreach** via calls, letters, and texts.

3. **Facilitate scheduling** of screening appointments.

4. **Track screening completion** and ensure follow-up for abnormal results.

5. **Coordinate care transitions** and address logistical barriers.

### Community Health Workers (CHWs)

1. **Provide culturally and linguistically tailored education** about preventive screenings.

2. **Conduct home visits or community outreach** to engage hard-to-reach populations.

3. **Deliver reminders** and offer navigation support.

4. **Help patients overcome barriers** related to transportation, health literacy, and healthcare system mistrust.

5. **Serve as liaisons** between patients and the healthcare system.

### Pharmacists

1. **Screen for social determinants of health** during medication reviews.

2. **Educate patients** about preventive screenings.

3. **Refer or directly connect patients** to screening services.

4. **Leverage accessibility** to identify gaps and provide reminders.

5. **Use motivational interviewing** to encourage screening adherence.

### Pharmacy Technicians

1. **Support pharmacists** by conducting initial screenings.

2. **Assist with reminder systems** for preventive care.

3. **Help patients navigate insurance coverage** for preventive services.

### Nurse Care Managers

1. **Develop and implement care plans** that include preventive screening goals.

2. **Conduct risk assessments** to identify high-priority screenings.

3. **Provide education and counseling** about the importance of preventive care.

4. **Monitor screening adherence** and follow up on missed appointments.

5. **Coordinate care** across multiple providers and settings.

### Social Workers

1. **Assess social determinants of health** that may impact screening adherence.

2. **Connect patients to community resources** for transportation, childcare, and other needs.

3. **Address financial barriers** through insurance enrollment assistance and connection to financial support programs.

4. **Provide emotional support** for patients anxious about screening procedures.

5. **Advocate for patients** within the healthcare system.

## Implementation Considerations

1. **Focus on cancer screening** where evidence is strongest, while developing approaches for other preventive services.

2. **Adapt interventions** for real-world, resource-limited environments.

3. **Address disparities** by tailoring approaches to disadvantaged populations.

4. **Evaluate effectiveness** across diverse populations and preventive care domains.
