## Specific Bite-Sized Recommendations

### 1. Structured Medication Therapy Management for Diabetes
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a comprehensive medication therapy management protocol for patients with diabetes that includes: 1) Systematic review of all medications including prescription, over-the-counter, and supplements; 2) Assessment of medication appropriateness using current guidelines; 3) Evaluation of medication adherence using standardized tools; 4) Identification of drug therapy problems including inappropriate dosing, drug interactions, and adverse effects; 5) Development of a personalized care plan with specific medication-related goals; and 6) Regular follow-up at 2-4 week intervals until glycemic targets are achieved, then every 3 months. Document all interventions and clinical outcomes including changes in HbA1c, blood pressure, lipids, and achievement of individualized goals.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All patients with diabetes, particularly those with suboptimal glycemic control (HbA1c > 7%) or complex medication regimens

### 2. Guideline-Directed Medication Selection Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a protocol for evidence-based medication selection in type 2 diabetes that includes: 1) Stratifying patients based on presence of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure, chronic kidney disease, or high risk for these conditions; 2) Prioritizing GLP-1 receptor agonists or SGLT2 inhibitors with proven cardiovascular or renal benefits for appropriate patients; 3) Considering patient-specific factors including hypoglycemia risk, weight effects, cost, and administration route; 4) Documenting rationale for medication selection and therapeutic goals; 5) Monitoring for efficacy and adverse effects at appropriate intervals; and 6) Adjusting therapy based on response and changing patient factors. The protocol should include specific criteria for when to initiate, intensify, or de-intensify therapy based on individualized HbA1c targets.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Patients with type 2 diabetes requiring pharmacotherapy beyond metformin

### 3. Insulin Management Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a structured insulin management protocol that includes: 1) Comprehensive patient education on insulin administration, storage, and hypoglycemia management; 2) Systematic dose titration based on patient-reported blood glucose patterns with specific adjustment algorithms; 3) Weekly follow-up during initiation and dose adjustment phases; 4) Standardized documentation of blood glucose patterns, insulin doses, and hypoglycemic events; 5) Regular assessment of injection technique and rotation sites; and 6) Continuous monitoring for barriers to optimal insulin use. The protocol should include specific guidance for different insulin regimens (basal only, basal-bolus, premixed) and situations requiring more intensive monitoring.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Patients with type 1 or type 2 diabetes requiring insulin therapy

### 4. Cardiovascular Risk Reduction in Diabetes
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a comprehensive cardiovascular risk reduction protocol for patients with diabetes that includes: 1) Systematic assessment of ASCVD risk using validated risk calculators; 2) Evidence-based statin selection and dosing based on risk category; 3) Blood pressure management targeting individualized goals based on comorbidities; 4) Appropriate use of antiplatelet therapy based on risk-benefit assessment; 5) Lifestyle modification counseling including smoking cessation, physical activity, and dietary approaches; and 6) Regular monitoring of lipid panels, blood pressure, and medication adherence. Document all interventions and clinical outcomes including changes in lipid parameters, blood pressure, and achievement of individualized goals.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All patients with diabetes, with emphasis on those with established ASCVD or multiple risk factors

### 5. Chronic Kidney Disease Management in Diabetes
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a protocol for managing diabetic kidney disease that includes: 1) Regular monitoring of estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR); 2) Implementation of RAAS inhibitor therapy when indicated with appropriate monitoring; 3) Initiation of SGLT2 inhibitors in appropriate patients with monitoring for side effects; 4) Medication dosage adjustments based on kidney function; 5) Avoidance of nephrotoxic medications; and 6) Patient education on kidney protection strategies. The protocol should include specific criteria for when to refer to nephrology and guidance on managing medications during acute kidney injury.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Patients with diabetes and evidence of kidney disease (reduced eGFR or albuminuria)

### 6. Hypertension Management Protocol for Patients with Diabetes
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a hypertension management protocol for patients with diabetes that includes: 1) Accurate blood pressure measurement techniques including home blood pressure monitoring; 2) Individualized blood pressure targets based on age, comorbidities, and risk factors; 3) Evidence-based medication selection with preference for ACE inhibitors or ARBs as first-line therapy; 4) Systematic dose titration with follow-up within 2-4 weeks of medication changes; 5) Monitoring for adverse effects including electrolyte abnormalities and acute kidney injury; and 6) Lifestyle modification counseling including DASH diet, sodium restriction, and physical activity. Document all interventions and clinical outcomes including changes in blood pressure and achievement of individualized goals.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Patients with diabetes and hypertension

### 7. Comprehensive Lipid Management Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a comprehensive lipid management protocol for patients with diabetes that includes: 1) Risk stratification based on age, ASCVD history, and risk factors; 2) Evidence-based statin selection and dosing based on risk category; 3) Non-statin therapy consideration for high-risk patients not achieving LDL-C goals on maximally tolerated statin therapy; 4) Systematic monitoring of lipid panels at appropriate intervals; 5) Proactive management of statin-associated side effects; and 6) Patient education on lifestyle modifications to improve lipid parameters. The protocol should include specific follow-up intervals based on therapy changes and risk category.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All patients with diabetes, with emphasis on those with established ASCVD or multiple risk factors

### 8. Hypoglycemia Risk Reduction Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a hypoglycemia risk reduction protocol that includes: 1) Systematic screening for hypoglycemia risk factors including age, kidney function, duration of diabetes, and history of severe hypoglycemia; 2) Medication regimen review with de-intensification when appropriate; 3) Individualized glycemic targets based on hypoglycemia risk; 4) Patient and caregiver education on hypoglycemia recognition, treatment, and prevention; 5) Prescription of glucagon for high-risk patients with training for caregivers; and 6) Regular reassessment of hypoglycemia frequency and severity. The protocol should include specific guidance for addressing nocturnal and asymptomatic hypoglycemia.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Patients with diabetes on insulin or insulin secretagogues, particularly older adults and those with impaired kidney function

### 9. Heart Failure Management in Diabetes
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a protocol for managing heart failure in patients with diabetes that includes: 1) Optimization of guideline-directed medical therapy for heart failure with specific attention to medication sequencing and titration; 2) Preferential use of diabetes medications with proven heart failure benefits (SGLT2 inhibitors) and avoidance of those with potential harm (thiazolidinediones); 3) Careful monitoring of volume status and kidney function; 4) Medication reconciliation to identify and resolve drug interactions; 5) Patient education on self-monitoring for heart failure symptoms and when to seek medical attention; and 6) Coordination with cardiology for complex cases. The protocol should include specific guidance on managing diabetes medications during heart failure exacerbations.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Patients with diabetes and heart failure

### 10. Antiplatelet Therapy Management Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement an antiplatelet therapy management protocol for patients with diabetes that includes: 1) Risk stratification for cardiovascular events and bleeding; 2) Evidence-based selection of antiplatelet agents based on comorbidities and risk factors; 3) Appropriate duration of dual antiplatelet therapy after acute coronary syndrome or percutaneous coronary intervention; 4) Systematic monitoring for bleeding complications; 5) Perioperative management strategies; and 6) Patient education on importance of adherence and bleeding precautions. The protocol should include specific guidance on managing antiplatelet therapy in patients requiring anticoagulation.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients with diabetes and established ASCVD or multiple cardiovascular risk factors

### 11. Comprehensive Medication Adherence Program
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a comprehensive medication adherence program for patients with diabetes that includes: 1) Systematic assessment of adherence using validated tools; 2) Identification of specific adherence barriers including cost, regimen complexity, side effects, and health beliefs; 3) Implementation of tailored interventions based on identified barriers; 4) Medication regimen simplification when possible; 5) Use of adherence aids including pill boxes, reminder systems, and technology solutions; and 6) Regular reassessment of adherence and intervention effectiveness. The program should include specific protocols for addressing common adherence barriers in diabetes care.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All patients with diabetes on chronic medications, particularly those with suboptimal outcomes or complex regimens

### 12. Pharmacist-Led Diabetes Self-Management Education
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured diabetes self-management education program led by clinical pharmacists that includes: 1) Comprehensive medication education including mechanism of action, administration techniques, and side effect management; 2) Training on blood glucose monitoring with interpretation of patterns; 3) Hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia recognition and management; 4) Sick day management protocols; 5) Nutrition and physical activity guidance; and 6) Goal setting and action planning. The program should include both group and individual sessions with regular follow-up to reinforce concepts and address emerging questions.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All patients with diabetes, particularly those newly diagnosed or starting new therapies

### 13. Transitions of Care Protocol for Diabetes
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a transitions of care protocol for patients with diabetes that includes: 1) Medication reconciliation within 24-48 hours of care transitions; 2) Assessment of post-discharge medication access and affordability; 3) Adjustment of diabetes regimen based on changes in clinical status, kidney function, or nutritional intake; 4) Communication with primary care providers regarding medication changes; 5) Patient education on any medication changes with teach-back verification; and 6) Follow-up within 3-7 days of discharge for high-risk patients. The protocol should include specific guidance for managing insulin regimens during transitions and criteria for high-risk patient identification.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients with diabetes transitioning between care settings, particularly those with recent medication changes or complex regimens

### 14. Continuous Glucose Monitoring Implementation Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a protocol for implementing continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) that includes: 1) Patient selection based on clinical indications and insurance coverage; 2) Comprehensive device training including insertion, calibration, and troubleshooting; 3) Structured education on data interpretation focusing on patterns and trends; 4) Development of specific action plans based on CGM data; 5) Regular follow-up to review data and adjust therapy; and 6) Assessment of ongoing barriers to effective CGM use. The protocol should include specific guidance on using time in range, glucose management indicator, and glycemic variability metrics to guide therapy decisions.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Patients with type 1 diabetes and selected patients with type 2 diabetes, particularly those on intensive insulin therapy or with problematic hypoglycemia

### 15. Diabetes Technology Management Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a diabetes technology management protocol that includes: 1) Assessment of patient readiness and suitability for insulin pumps and automated insulin delivery systems; 2) Comprehensive device training and troubleshooting support; 3) Regular data downloads and analysis with structured documentation; 4) Systematic adjustment of pump settings based on glucose patterns; 5) Coordination with endocrinology for complex cases; and 6) Regular reassessment of technology needs and effectiveness. The protocol should include specific guidance on managing sick days, exercise, and other special situations with diabetes technology.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients using insulin pumps, automated insulin delivery systems, or other diabetes technologies

### 16. Pharmacist-Led Diabetes Population Health Management
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a population health management program for diabetes that includes: 1) Regular data analysis to identify patients with care gaps or suboptimal control; 2) Risk stratification to prioritize outreach efforts; 3) Proactive outreach to high-risk patients; 4) Standardized interventions for common care gaps; 5) Tracking of clinical outcomes and quality measures; and 6) Regular reporting to stakeholders on program impact. The program should include specific protocols for addressing common quality measures including HbA1c testing, eye examinations, kidney function monitoring, and statin use.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All patients with diabetes within a defined population or practice

### 17. Diabetes Complication Screening Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a comprehensive diabetes complication screening protocol that includes: 1) Systematic tracking of recommended screenings including retinopathy, neuropathy, nephropathy, and cardiovascular assessments; 2) Proactive identification of patients due for screenings; 3) Facilitation of referrals and appointments; 4) Documentation of screening results; 5) Communication of abnormal findings to primary care providers; and 6) Patient education on the importance of regular screenings. The protocol should include specific guidance on screening frequency based on risk factors and previous findings.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All patients with diabetes

### 18. Pharmacogenomic-Guided Medication Selection
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a protocol for pharmacogenomic-guided medication selection in diabetes that includes: 1) Identification of patients who may benefit from pharmacogenomic testing; 2) Appropriate test ordering and interpretation; 3) Evidence-based medication selection and dosing based on genetic results; 4) Documentation of genetic results in the medical record; 5) Patient education on implications of genetic findings; and 6) Regular reassessment as new evidence emerges. The protocol should include specific guidance on interpreting and applying results for commonly tested genes affecting diabetes medications.

**Evidence Level:** C - Limited
**Target Population:** Selected patients with diabetes, particularly those with history of medication intolerance or inadequate response to standard therapy

### 19. Obesity Management in Diabetes
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish an obesity management protocol for patients with diabetes that includes: 1) Regular assessment of body mass index and waist circumference; 2) Evidence-based selection of anti-obesity medications with consideration of effects on glycemic control; 3) Systematic monitoring of weight loss progress and medication side effects; 4) Coordination with dietitians and behavioral health providers; 5) Patient education on lifestyle modifications to support weight management; and 6) Regular reassessment of therapy effectiveness. The protocol should include specific guidance on selecting diabetes medications with favorable weight effects.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients with diabetes and obesity (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² or ≥ 27 kg/m² with weight-related comorbidities)

### 20. Collaborative Care Model for Complex Diabetes
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a collaborative care model for complex diabetes that includes: 1) Regular case conferences with multidisciplinary team members; 2) Structured communication protocols between team members; 3) Clear delineation of roles and responsibilities; 4) Shared documentation systems; 5) Coordinated care planning with patient involvement; and 6) Regular assessment of team effectiveness and patient outcomes. The model should include specific protocols for escalation of care when patients are not meeting goals despite standard interventions.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients with complex diabetes needs including multiple complications, comorbidities, or psychosocial barriers
