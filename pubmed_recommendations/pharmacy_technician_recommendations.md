# Pharmacy Technician Medication Adherence Best Practices Recommendations

Citations:

## Specific Bite-Sized Recommendations

### 1. Medication Synchronization Program Implementation
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured medication synchronization program where pharmacy technicians coordinate the refill schedule for all of a patient's chronic medications to be filled on the same day each month. The program should include: 1) Identifying eligible patients through prescription refill data analysis; 2) Conducting initial patient outreach to explain the program benefits; 3) Performing a comprehensive medication inventory and reconciliation; 4) Calculating appropriate short fills to align medication schedules; 5) Setting up a consistent monthly pickup or delivery date; and 6) Establishing a proactive monthly contact protocol to address any medication changes or issues before the scheduled fill date. Pharmacy technicians should document all patient interactions and maintain a dedicated calendar system for tracking synchronized patients.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Patients taking multiple chronic medications, particularly older adults and those with complex medication regimens

### 2. Structured Refill Reminder System
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a comprehensive refill reminder system managed by pharmacy technicians that includes: 1) Setting up automated text, phone, or email reminders based on patient preference; 2) Implementing a tiered approach with initial reminders 7 days before due date, followed by secondary reminders 3 days before; 3) Documenting all reminder attempts and patient responses in a standardized format; 4) Flagging non-responsive patients for direct outreach; 5) Creating a protocol for escalation to pharmacist intervention after two unsuccessful reminder cycles; and 6) Analyzing reminder response data monthly to identify trends and opportunities for system improvement. The system should include options for patients to respond directly to confirm, decline, or request modifications to their refills.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** All patients on maintenance medications, particularly those with history of late refills

### 3. Medication Adherence Packaging Services
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a medication adherence packaging service where pharmacy technicians prepare customized adherence packaging (e.g., blister packs, medication boxes, or strip packaging) with: 1) Standardized workflow for identifying eligible patients based on adherence metrics and medication complexity; 2) Comprehensive medication review and reconciliation before initiating packaging; 3) Clear documentation of all medications included and excluded from packaging; 4) Consistent labeling system with large print, color coding, and timing indicators; 5) Regular quality control checks requiring dual verification; and 6) Scheduled follow-up calls after initial packaging and then monthly to assess effectiveness and address any issues. Technicians should maintain detailed records of all packaging configurations and changes over time.

**Evidence Level:** A - Strong
**Target Population:** Patients with complex medication regimens, cognitive impairments, or demonstrated adherence challenges

### 4. Patient Medication Profile Review Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a protocol for pharmacy technicians to conduct structured medication profile reviews that includes: 1) Reviewing patient medication history for patterns of late refills or gaps in therapy; 2) Calculating medication possession ratios (MPRs) or proportion of days covered (PDC) for key maintenance medications; 3) Flagging patients with MPR/PDC below 80% for targeted interventions; 4) Documenting specific adherence barriers identified during patient conversations; 5) Categorizing barriers according to a standardized taxonomy (e.g., cost, side effects, forgetfulness, health beliefs); and 6) Routing findings to pharmacists for clinical intervention. The protocol should include a standardized documentation template and be conducted at least quarterly for high-risk patients.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All patients on chronic medications, with priority for those with complex regimens or high-risk conditions

### 5. Targeted Adherence Outreach Program
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a targeted outreach program where pharmacy technicians contact patients with identified adherence issues through: 1) Generating weekly reports of patients with late refills (>7 days) for chronic medications; 2) Conducting structured phone interviews using a standardized script to identify specific adherence barriers; 3) Documenting barriers using a consistent classification system; 4) Providing first-line solutions within technician scope (e.g., refill synchronization, reminder systems, affordability resources); 5) Escalating complex issues to pharmacists using a warm handoff approach; and 6) Scheduling follow-up contacts based on risk stratification. All interactions should be documented in the patient's profile with specific adherence barriers and interventions noted.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients identified with suboptimal medication adherence through refill data or clinical metrics

### 6. Medication Cost Reduction Intervention
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a systematic approach for pharmacy technicians to address medication cost barriers through: 1) Screening all new prescriptions and refills for potential cost-saving opportunities; 2) Maintaining an up-to-date database of patient assistance programs, discount cards, and manufacturer coupons; 3) Proactively identifying therapeutic alternatives that may be less expensive; 4) Preparing cost comparison information for pharmacist review; 5) Assisting patients with enrollment in eligible assistance programs; and 6) Documenting all cost interventions and resulting savings in the patient record. Technicians should receive regular training updates on new assistance programs and be able to help patients complete necessary paperwork.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients expressing cost concerns or showing adherence gaps potentially related to medication costs

### 7. Standardized New Patient Medication Education Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a standardized protocol for pharmacy technicians to support new patient medication education through: 1) Preparing personalized medication information sheets with key points highlighted for pharmacist counseling; 2) Setting up patients in adherence support systems before they leave the pharmacy; 3) Demonstrating proper use of adherence tools (e.g., pill boxes, reminder apps); 4) Scheduling follow-up calls 3-7 days after new therapy initiation to identify early challenges; 5) Documenting specific questions or concerns for pharmacist attention; and 6) Creating a "new start" checklist that ensures all adherence support steps are completed. The protocol should include specific guidance for different medication classes and common adherence challenges.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients starting new chronic medications, particularly those with complex regimens or high-risk medications

### 8. Technology-Enabled Adherence Monitoring
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a technology-enabled adherence monitoring system where pharmacy technicians: 1) Help patients set up medication reminder apps or smart pill bottles during prescription pickup; 2) Conduct brief training sessions on using adherence technology tools; 3) Monitor incoming adherence data from connected devices or apps; 4) Contact patients when the system flags missed doses or unusual patterns; 5) Document technology-reported adherence rates in the patient record; and 6) Troubleshoot technical issues to ensure continuous monitoring. The protocol should include regular data review sessions and escalation pathways for persistent adherence issues.

**Evidence Level:** C - Limited
**Target Population:** Tech-savvy patients or those with caregivers comfortable with digital tools, particularly those with adherence challenges

### 9. Post-Discharge Medication Reconciliation Support
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a post-discharge medication support program where pharmacy technicians: 1) Receive discharge medication lists from local hospitals; 2) Compare discharge medications with pre-hospitalization regimens to identify changes; 3) Prepare a comprehensive medication change report for pharmacist review; 4) Contact patients within 72 hours of discharge to confirm understanding of medication changes; 5) Coordinate with prescribers to resolve discrepancies; and 6) Schedule follow-up calls at 7 and 30 days post-discharge to address emerging adherence issues. The program should include standardized documentation templates and clear escalation pathways for clinical concerns.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients recently discharged from hospitals or care facilities, particularly those with medication regimen changes

### 10. Culturally Responsive Adherence Support
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a culturally responsive adherence support program where pharmacy technicians: 1) Receive training on cultural factors affecting medication use in predominant community populations; 2) Use culturally appropriate assessment tools to identify specific adherence barriers; 3) Provide medication information in the patient's preferred language; 4) Incorporate cultural beliefs and practices into adherence strategies; 5) Connect patients with culturally concordant community resources when available; and 6) Document cultural factors relevant to medication adherence in the patient record. The program should include regular cultural competency training updates and community engagement components.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients from diverse cultural backgrounds, particularly those with limited English proficiency or strong cultural health beliefs

### 11. Adherence Data Tracking and Reporting System
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a comprehensive adherence data tracking system where pharmacy technicians: 1) Generate monthly adherence reports for key medication classes (e.g., antihypertensives, antidiabetics, statins); 2) Calculate standardized adherence metrics (PDC, MPR) for individual patients and population segments; 3) Identify trends and patterns in non-adherence; 4) Flag high-risk patients for targeted interventions; 5) Track intervention outcomes over time; and 6) Prepare summary reports for pharmacy quality improvement meetings. The system should include visualization tools to easily identify adherence patterns and intervention opportunities.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All patients on chronic medications, with pharmacy-level analysis to identify systemic improvement opportunities

### 12. Caregiver Engagement Protocol
**Implementation Guidance:** Establish a structured caregiver engagement protocol where pharmacy technicians: 1) Identify patients who rely on caregivers for medication management; 2) Document authorized caregiver contact information and relationship; 3) Include caregivers in medication synchronization and reminder systems; 4) Provide caregivers with specific training on adherence tools and strategies; 5) Schedule regular check-ins with caregivers to address emerging challenges; and 6) Create caregiver-specific educational materials addressing common medication management issues. The protocol should include guidance for navigating privacy considerations while maximizing caregiver support.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** Patients with cognitive impairments, complex needs, or who rely on caregivers for medication management

### 13. Pharmacy Technician-Led Adherence Consultation Program
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a structured adherence consultation program where specially trained pharmacy technicians: 1) Conduct scheduled 15-20 minute adherence consultations in a private area; 2) Use a standardized assessment tool to identify specific adherence barriers; 3) Apply motivational interviewing techniques to explore adherence challenges; 4) Collaborate with patients to develop personalized adherence strategies; 5) Document consultation findings and recommendations; and 6) Schedule follow-up consultations to assess strategy effectiveness. The program should include clear boundaries for technician scope and protocols for pharmacist referral when clinical issues arise.

**Evidence Level:** C - Limited
**Target Population:** Patients identified with adherence challenges who would benefit from dedicated consultation time

### 14. Standardized Communication Protocol for Prescriber Coordination
**Implementation Guidance:** Develop a standardized protocol for pharmacy technicians to support prescriber coordination on adherence issues through: 1) Preparing adherence reports for prescriber offices highlighting specific patient adherence concerns; 2) Establishing dedicated communication channels with prescriber office staff; 3) Facilitating information exchange about adherence barriers identified in the pharmacy; 4) Coordinating medication synchronization with prescriber appointment schedules; 5) Documenting all prescriber communications regarding adherence; and 6) Following up on action items from prescriber communications. The protocol should include templates for different types of prescriber communications and clear guidance on scope of information sharing.

**Evidence Level:** C - Limited
**Target Population:** Patients with adherence issues requiring prescriber intervention or coordination

### 15. Adherence Intervention Documentation and Follow-Up System
**Implementation Guidance:** Implement a comprehensive documentation and follow-up system where pharmacy technicians: 1) Document all adherence interventions using standardized terminology and templates; 2) Categorize interventions by type, intensity, and targeted barrier; 3) Schedule appropriate follow-up based on intervention type and patient risk level; 4) Track intervention outcomes using defined metrics; 5) Identify patterns of successful and unsuccessful interventions; and 6) Use data to continuously refine the adherence program. The system should include automated follow-up reminders and escalation protocols for unsuccessful interventions.

**Evidence Level:** B - Moderate
**Target Population:** All patients receiving adherence interventions, with emphasis on ensuring appropriate follow-up and outcome tracking
